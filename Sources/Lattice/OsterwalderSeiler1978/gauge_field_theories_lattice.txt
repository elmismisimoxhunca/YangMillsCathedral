          We begin a rigorous, nonperturbative investigation of quantum field theories with local
       internal symmetries. We discuss the lattice approximation of Yang-Mills fields and offer-
       mion fields in the Euclidean setup and we verify physical positivity for the Schwinger
       functions of these approximations. This implies the existence of a positive self-adjoint
       transfer matrix. We then prove existence and analyticity of the infinite volume limit of
       strongly coupled Yang-Mills theories on the lattice and we verify Wilson’s confinement
       bound. Finally we present a rigorous treatment of the Higgs mechanism in lattice gauge
       theories.


                                                      1. TNTR~DuCTI~N

   Quantum field theories with (non-abelian) local internal symmetry groups appear
to be the most promising models for elementary particles interacting weakly, electro-
magnetically, and strongly. While there is a large classof quantum field theory models,
without local internal symmetries (and therefore not containing gauge fields), which
we think we understand quite well both on a perturbative and on a nonperturbative
level, there is much less that is known about the mathematical structure of gauge
field theories. On the level of formal power seriesexpansions, much work has been
done to show that gauge theories are renormalizable; see[l, 20, 341.
   In this paper we begin an analysis of gauge field theories on a nonperturbative
level. The general strategy is to regularize the theory through the introduction of
cutoffs and to study subsequently the limit as the cutoffs are removed. Unfortunately
the cutoffs always destroy some of the symmetries of the models, which then have
to be recovered in the limit. Also, some cutoffs destroy the positivity of the metric

                           2. THE LATTICE APPROXIMATION

   Euclidean Green’s functions of an arbitrary fermion-boson       model uniquely define
a relativistic quantum field theory if they satisfy the conditions of physical positivity,
(anti)symmetry,    Euclidean covariance, and a regularity condition [25].
   In this section we introduce a lattice approximation      for Euclidean quantum field
theories, involving gauge fields, fermions, and bosons, and show that it satisfies the
physical positivity condition (i.e., positive metric for the Hilbert space of physical
states). This condition will continue to hold in the continuum limit, i.e., as we let the
lattice spacing tend to zero. Following the general scheme introduced in [25] we then
construct the Hamiltonian H, or the transfer matrix T = e-H, in the language of
statistical mechanics. It follows from the construction that H = H* > 0.

gauge fields is standard, too [2,35], whereas we treat the fermions somewhat differently

inside. See also [22].

explains why in a continuum theory (for instance, free QED) gauge invariance is
always broken (see [33]).
