         Euclidean Yang-Mills theory by interpreting it as a stochastic differential equation in the

                                                      1. INTRODUCTION

   This paper presents an analysis of quantized Euclidean Yang-Mills gauge theory
in two space time dimensions. The theory is formulated in the continuum without
a cutoff. This is possible because in complete axial gauge the Euclidean Yang-Mills
action defines a Gaussian measure for the gauge field. The long range goal of our
analysis is to explore the regularity properties of new observables called “lassos”
which have been considered before in several contexts [6, 7, l&12, 14-18, 2&22,
25-29, 31-33, 351. In most formulations            of quantized Yang-Mills  theory, the
Wilson loop variables (i.e., holonomy group elements) play a fundamental role. It
is the expectation of products of these observables that we shall investigate in this
paper in preparation.for an analysis of the lassos in a later work. It is expected that
the Wilson loop variables are highly singular in three and four space time dimen-
sions and that averaging over paths will not improve the situation. In contrast there
is reason to believe that the lassos will be more regular in this respect. Our main
tool in this article is the stochastic differential calculus.
   We first show that the differential equation for parallel translation along a
smooth curve in R2 with respect to a typical gauge potential A can be intrepreted
as a stochastic differential equation. This relies on our use of a gauge for which the
Yang-Mills action is Gaussian. We compute the expectation of products of Wilson
loops using the beautiful technique of N. Bralic [ 111. We show that Bralic’s method
takes on its proper mathematical meaning in the context of stochastic differential
calculus. It has long been recognized [23] that the expectation of Wilson loop
