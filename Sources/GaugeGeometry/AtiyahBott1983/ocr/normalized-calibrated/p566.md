If $D_{i}$ has rank $n_{i}$ and Chern class $k_{i}$ so that $n = \sum n_{i}, k = \sum k_{i}$ we shall call the sequence of pairs

$\left(n_{i}, k_{i}\right) i = 1 , \ldots , r$ the type of $E$. It is sometimes convenient to describe the type equivalently by

the single $n$-vector $\mu$ whose components are the ratios $k_{i}/ n_{i}$ each represented $n_{i}$ times and arranged

in decreasing order. Thus

\[ \mu = \left(\mu_{1}, \ldots , \mu_{n}\right) \]

with $\mu_{1}\geqslant \mu_{2}\geqslant \ldots \geqslant \mu_{n}$, where the first $n_{i}$ are equal to $k_{1}/ n_{1}$, the next $n_{2}$ are equal to $k_{2}/ n_{2}$ and

so on.

[?line conf=0.91: All the holomorphic bundles of a given type $\mu$ define a subspace $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$ of $\mathscr{C}$. In particular if all]

components of $\mu$ are equal (hence are all $k / n$ ) then $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}= \mathscr{C}_{\text{ss}}$ is the semi-stable part of $\mathscr{C}$.

Since the filtration (7.1) is canonical the subspaces $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$ are preserved by the action of the

[?line conf=0.77: automorphism group, so that each $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$ is a union of orbits.]

It is well known that the infinitesimal variations of a holomorphic bundle $E$ are classified by

the elements of the sheaf cohomology group $H^{1}( M , \operatorname{End}E )$. In our picture this gets interpreted

as follows. The orbit in $\mathscr{C}$ corresponding to a given holomorphic bundle $E$ is, locally, a manifold

of finite codimension in $\mathscr{C}$ and its normal can be identified with $H^{1}( M , \operatorname{End}E )$. This is because

[?line conf=0.93: an infinitesimal gauge transformation, namely a global $C^{\infty}$ endomorphism $\phi$ of $E$, alters d" by]

the addition of $\mathrm{d}^{\prime \prime}\phi$ and the cokernel of

\[ \Omega^{0}( \operatorname{End}E ) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{d}^{\prime \prime}}\Omega^{0,1}( \operatorname{End}E ) \]

is just $H^{1}( M , \operatorname{End}E )$.

[?line conf=0.65: In the same way we can identify the conormal to $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$. Since $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$ is a union of orbits its conormal]

[?line conf=0.86: should be a quotient of $H^{1}( M , \operatorname{End}E )$. Now let $\operatorname{End}'E$ denote the bundle of holomorphic endo-]

morphisms of $E$ that preserve its canonical filtration and define $\operatorname{End}^{\prime \prime}E$ by the exact sequence

(7.2)

\[ 0 \rightarrow \operatorname{End}'E \rightarrow \operatorname{End}E \rightarrow \operatorname{End}^{\prime \prime}E \rightarrow 0 . \]

From the exact cohomology sequence of (7.2) we see that $H^{1}\left(M , \operatorname{End}^{\prime \prime}E\right)$ is indeed a quotient of

[?line conf=0.92: $H^{1}( M , \operatorname{End}E )$ and this is clearly the right candidate for the conormal to $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$, since $H^{1}\left(M , \operatorname{End}'E\right)$]

describes variation inside $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$. The important point to notice at this stage is

(7.3)

[?line conf=0.56: \[ \operatorname{dim}H^{1}\left(M , \operatorname{End}^{\prime \prime}E\right) \quad \text{depends only on}\mu . \]]

This follows from Riemann-Roch together with the key fact that

(7.4)

\[ H^{0}\left(M , \operatorname{End}^{\prime \prime}E\right) = 0 . \]

This in turn is an easy corollary, by induction, of

(7.5)

\[ \text{If}E , D \text{are both semi-stable and}\mu ( E ) > \mu ( D ) \text{then every homomorphism}E \rightarrow D \text{is zero.}\]

The proof of (7.5) is a simple consequence of the definitions and can be deduced from

Narasimhan \& Seshadri (1965, proposition 4.4): it is in any case an essential step in the proof of

the uniqueness of the canonical filtration.

From (7.3) we can deduce that $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$ is locally a submanifold of finite codimension in $\mathscr{C}$. Thus the

generalized cell-structure. To understand the mutual positions of the $\mathscr{C}_{\mu}$ we need to know some-
