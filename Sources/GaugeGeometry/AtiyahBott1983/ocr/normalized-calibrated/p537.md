Hence the transitive relation < generated by < is a partial ordering and has the property that

(1.13)

\[ \text{closure of}M^{+}( N ) \subset \bigcup_{N'\geqslant N}M^{+}\left(N'\right) \text{.}\]

Sometimes one may be given an explicit finite stratification of $M$

(1.14)

\[ M = \bigcup_{\lambda}M_{\lambda}, \]

where each $M_{\lambda}$ is a locally closed submanifold of $M$, and the index set of $\lambda$ is partially ordered so

that

(1.15)

\[ \bar{M}_{\lambda}\subset \bigcup_{\mu \geqslant \lambda}M_{\mu}\]

holds for all $\lambda$ (we assume the partial ordering is strict, i.e. $\lambda \leqslant \mu$ and $\mu \leqslant \lambda$ implies $\lambda = \mu$ )

One can then use the stratification to get Morse-type information about the homology of $M$.

We start with the open strata, given by minimal $\lambda$, and inductively add other strata. At each

stage we can write down the exact cohomology sequence for a pair ( $U , U - V$ ) where $V$ is a

closed submanifold of $U$. More formally this can be described as follows. Define a subset $I$ of

indices to be

\[ \begin{array}{l l}\text{open}& \text{if}\lambda \in I \text{and}\mu \leqslant \lambda \quad \Rightarrow \mu \in I \\ \text{closed}& \text{if}\lambda \in I \text{and}\mu \geqslant \lambda \quad \Rightarrow \mu \in I . \end{array}\]

It is easy to check that $I$ is closed if and only if its complement $I'$ is open. Moreover the subspace

of $M$ defined by

\[ M_{I}= \bigcup_{\lambda \in I}M_{\lambda}\]

is open or (or closed) if $I$ is open (or closed): this follows from (1.15). If $I$ is open and $\lambda \in I'$ is

minimal then $J = I \cup \lambda$ is open, and our inductive step is from $M_{I}$ to $M_{J}$. From (1.15) it follows

that $M_{\lambda}= M_{J}- M_{I}$ is a closed submanifold of $M_{J}$. Assuming for simplicity that the normal

bundles to all strata in $M$ are orientable we have the exact sequence

(1.16)

\[ \rightarrow H^{q - k}\left(M_{\lambda}\right) \rightarrow H^{q}\left(M_{J}\right) \rightarrow H^{q}\left(M_{I}\right) \rightarrow , \]

where we have used the Thom isomorphism

\[ H^{q - k}\left(M_{\lambda}\right) \cong H^{q}\left(M_{J}, M_{I}\right) \]

with $k = k_{\lambda}= \operatorname{codim}M_{\lambda}$.

If, for a given field $K$ of coefficients, (1.16) breaks up into short exact sequences for all $q$ and

all $\lambda$ it follows that

[?line conf=0.87: \[ P_{t}( M ) = \sum t^{k_{\lambda}}P_{t}\left(M_{\lambda}\right) . \]]

In such a case we shall say that the stratification is perfect over $K$. If this holds for $K = Z_{p}$, for all

primes $p$, we shall simply call it perfect. Thus a perfect Morse function defines a perfect stratifi-

cation.

If the stratification is $G$-invariant and the corresponding equivariant cohomology sequences

break up we shall call the stratification equivariantly or $G$-perfect. Proposition 1.9 has an obvious

analogue in this context with the normal bundle in question being the normal to a stratum.
