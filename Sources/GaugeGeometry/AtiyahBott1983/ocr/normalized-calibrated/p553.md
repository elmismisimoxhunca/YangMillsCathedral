We write $\hat{F}$ for the endomorphism

\[ \hat{F}: \eta \rightarrow * [ * F , \eta ] \]

of $\Omega^{1}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$. This is clearly a degree-zero operator, which by the argument of (4.11) is

also characterized by

\[ ( \hat{F}\eta , \xi ) = ( F , [ \eta , \xi ] ) \]

and is therefore self-adjoint.

Remarks. The operator

\[ \mathrm{L}_{A}\equiv \mathrm{~d}_{A}^{*}\mathrm{~d}_{A}+ * [ * F , ] \]

which appears in (4.10), can also be interpreted as the Jacobi operator associated with an extremal

of $L ( A )$. That is, if $A_{t}= A + t \eta + t^{2}\eta'\ldots$ describes a curve of connections for which $L ( A )$ is

extremal, then

[?line conf=0.73: \[ \mathrm{L}_{A}\eta = 0 . \]]

To see this we differentiate the equations

\[ \mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}^{*}F \left(A_{t}\right) = 0 \]

with respect to $t$ and set $t = 0$. If a dot denotes such a differentiation then we clearly have

\[ \dot{\mathrm{d}}_{A}= \eta , \quad F ( \dot{A}) = \mathrm{d}_{A}\eta , \]

whence the derivative of

\[ * \mathrm{~d}_{A}* F ( A ) = 0 \]

is given by

\[ * [ \eta , * F ( A ) ] + * \mathrm{~d}_{A}* \mathrm{~d}_{A}\eta = 0 , \]

which, once the signs are taken care of, is equivalent to

(4.12)

\[ \mathrm{L}_{A}\eta = 0 , \quad \eta \in \Omega^{1}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) ) . \]

The solutions of (4.12) are therefore the 'Jacobi-fields' of $L$, and describe the tangent space to

the space of solutions of the nonlinear extremal equations for $L$.

On the other hand our functional $L$ is clearly invariant under the action of the group of gauge trans-

formations. Hence the proper measure of the tangent space to the space of solutions is given by

the quotient of the solutions of (4.12) by the directions along the orbits of the action of $\mathscr{G}( P )$.

Now this space is, as we shall show in a moment, precisely the image of $\Omega^{0}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$ in

$\Omega^{1}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$ under $\mathrm{d}_{A}$. Thus the corrected tangent space to the space of solutions is the quotient

[?line conf=0.93: $N_{A}$ of the space of Jacobi fields $J_{A}$ by the image of $\mathrm{d}_{A}$ and therefore fits into the exact sequence]

(4.13)

\[ \Omega^{0}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) ) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{d}_{A}}J_{A}\longrightarrow N_{A}\longrightarrow 0 . \]

We shall call $N_{A}$ the null space of $Q_{A}$, and its dimension the nullity of $A$. This nullity is always

finite because of the following argument.

In our norm on $\Omega^{1}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$ the orthocomplement of the image of the $\mathrm{d}_{A}$ in (4.13) is

precisely the kernel of $\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}$. Thus $N_{A}$ may be identified with the space of $\eta \in \Omega^{1}$ satisfying the

equations

[?line conf=0.88: \[ \mathrm{L}_{\Delta}\eta = 0 , \quad \mathrm{~d}_{A}^{*}\eta = 0 \]]

or equivalently

(4.14)

[?line conf=0.84: \[ \mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}\mathrm{~d}_{A}+ \mathrm{d}_{A}\mathrm{~d}_{A}^{*}+ * [ * F , ] = 0 , \quad \mathrm{~d}_{A}^{*}= 0 . \]]

[?line conf=0.62: The first operator on the left is the Laplacian $\Delta_{A}$ of $\mathrm{d}_{A}$ and hence elliptic. Hence the solutions of]

(4.14) are finite-dimensional, and therefore the nullity is also.
