M. F. ATIYAH AND R. BOTT

[?line conf=0.86: Proposition 2.4. Let $B G$ be the classifying space for $G$. Then in homotopy theory]

\[ B \mathscr{G}( P ) = \operatorname{Map}_{P}( M , B G ) . \]

Here the subscript $P$ denotes the component of a map of $M$ into $B G$ which induces $P$.

Proof. Let

\[ G \rightarrow E G \rightarrow B G \]

by a universal bundle for $G$, and consider the space $\operatorname{Map}_{G}( P , E )$ of $G$-equivariant maps of

$P$ to $E$.

The group $\mathscr{G}( P )$ now acts naturally on this space by composition, to yield the principal

fibring

\[ \mathscr{G}( P ) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Map}_{G}( P , E ) \xrightarrow{\pi}\operatorname{Map}_{P}( M , B G ) . \]

If $B G$ is paracompact and locally contractible, which is easily arranged, $\pi$ will be a locally trivial

principal fibring, as follows easily from the homotopy properties of fibrings. The total space

[?line conf=0.85: $\operatorname{Map}_{G}( P , E )$ is contractible so that this is a universal bundle for $\mathscr{G}( P )$, and]

\[ B \mathscr{G}( P ) = \operatorname{Map}_{P}( M , B G ) \]

as was asserted.

Using (2.4) we now compute $B \mathscr{G}( P )$ for the cases we have in mind.

[?line conf=0.68: Case I. The unitary group U(1)]

The group $U ( 1 )$ is the circle $S^{1}$ of complex numbers of norm 1 . These act naturally on the unit

sphere $S ( H )$ of a Hilbert space $H$ over $C$, and the quotient space

\[ P ( H ) = S ( H ) / S^{1}\]

is the projective space of rays in $H$. When $\operatorname{dim}H = \infty , S ( H )$ becomes contractible, and hence

(2.5)

\[ S^{1}\longrightarrow S ( H ) \xrightarrow{\pi}P ( H ) \]

is a universal $S^{1}$-bundle. From the corresponding exact homotopy sequence it now follows that

while

\[ \begin{aligned}& \pi_{k}\{P ( H ) \}= 0 \quad \text{for}\quad k \neq 2 \\ & \pi_{2}\{P ( H ) \}= Z \end{aligned}\]

Thus $P ( H )$, which is the $B G$ in this case, is an Eilenberg-Maclane space $K ( Z ; 2 )$. Now it is a

[?line conf=0.57: theorem of René Thom that, if $Y$ is such a space, and $X$ any finite complex, then Map $( X ; Y )$]

is again a product of such spaces. Precisely,

Theorem (Thom). Let $\pi_{q}( Y ) = 0$ for $q \neq n$ and let $\pi_{n}( Y ) = \pi$. Then

(2.6)

\[ \operatorname{Map}( X , Y ) = \prod_{q}K \left\{H^{q}( X , \pi ) ; n - q \right\}. \]

For a Riemann surface $M$ of genus $g$ this yields the corollary:

\[ \operatorname{Map}\left(M ; B S^{1}\right) = Z \times \underbrace{S^{1}\times \ldots \times S^{1}}_{2 g}\times P ( H ) \]

corresponding to the fact that

\[ \begin{aligned}& H^{1}( M ; Z ) \cong Z \oplus \ldots \oplus Z \quad ( 2 g \text{factors}) \\ & H^{0}( M ; Z ) \cong H^{2}( X ; Z ) \cong Z \end{aligned}\]
