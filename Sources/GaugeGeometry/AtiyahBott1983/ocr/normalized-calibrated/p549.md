Indeed, let $\tilde{X}, \tilde{Y}$ be $A$-horizontal lifts of $X , Y$ on $M$. Then

(3.8)

\[ \mathrm{d}\theta_{A}[ \tilde{X}, \tilde{Y}] = - \theta_{A}[ \tilde{X}, \tilde{Y}] \]

as the other terms such as $\tilde{X}\theta_{A}( \tilde{Y})$ disappear. On the other hand $\mathrm{d}\theta_{A}$ vanishes in the direction $Z$ :

\[ i ( Z ) \mathrm{d}\theta_{A}= 0 , \quad \mathscr{L}( Z ) \mathrm{d}\theta_{A}= 0 \]

as follows from (3.5) and (3.6) via the basic identity

\[ \mathscr{L}( Z ) = i ( Z ) \mathrm{d}+ \mathrm{d}i ( Z ) . \]

Hence (3.7) follows from (3.8).

Equation (3.7) implies that

\[ \mathrm{d}F ( A ) = 0 . \]

Furthermore, the formula (3.3) now yields

[?line conf=0.72: (3.9)]

\[ F ( A ) - F \left(A'\right) = \mathrm{d}\eta , \quad \eta \in \Omega^{1}( M ) . \]

Thus the map $A \rightarrow F ( A )$ sends $\mathscr{A}( P )$ precisely onto a certain cohomology class $k ( P ) \in H^{2}( M )$ :

\[ \mathscr{A}( P ) \xrightarrow{F}k ( P ) \longrightarrow 0 . \]

We next turn to the fibre of this map $F$. Again from (3.9) it follows that if $F ( A ) = F \left(A'\right)$, then

$\eta = A - A'$ is closed, and conversely. Thus the fibre of $F$ consists precisely of the space of closed

[?line conf=0.87: 1-forms $Z^{1}( M ) \in \Omega^{1}( M )$. Hence we have the 'exact sequence']

\[ 0 \rightarrow Z^{1}( M ) \rightarrow \mathscr{A}( P ) \rightarrow k ( P ) \rightarrow 0 , \]

which is unorthodox in that $\mathscr{A}( P )$ is only an affine space, and $k ( P )$ denotes the whole coset in $Z^{2}( M )$

[?line conf=0.86: representing a class usually denoted by $- 2 \pi \mathrm{i}c_{1}( P )$ in $H^{2}( M )$.]

[?line conf=0.81: We next describe the action of $\mathscr{G}( P )$ on this sequence. In the present instance Ad $P$ is clearly]

trivial, and hence

(3.10)

\[ \mathscr{G}( P ) \cong \operatorname{Map}\left(M , S^{1}\right) . \]

We now have

Lemma 3.11. If $f : M \rightarrow S^{1}$ is a smooth map, then its effect, via (3.10), on $\theta_{A}$ is given by

\[ f^{*}\theta_{A}= \theta_{A}+ \pi^{*}f^{*}v \]

where $v$ is the form on $S^{1}$ discussed earlier.

As a first consequence we see immediately that, because $v$ is closed, $F \left(f^{\#}A\right) = \mathrm{d}\theta_{A}= F ( A )$.

Thus $F$ is invariant under the gauge group.

Next we see that $\mathscr{G}( P )$ acts on $Z^{1}( M )$ by translations

\[ \xi \rightarrow \xi + f^{*}v . \]

Consider now the action of the identity component $\mathscr{G}_{0}( P )$ on $Z^{1}( M )$. Clearly such a map lifts

to a map $\tilde{f}$ in the diagram

[?line conf=0.80: \[ \stackrel{M}{\underset{f}{\stackrel{\tilde{f}}{\longrightarrow}}}\underset{S^{\downarrow}}{R}\]]

and if $x$ is the linear function on $R$ with

\[ \exp^{*}v = \mathrm{d}x \]

then

\[ f^{*}v = \mathrm{d}\tilde{f}. \]
