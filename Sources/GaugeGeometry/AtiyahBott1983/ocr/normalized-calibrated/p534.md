Note that for the fixed point $p$ (or $q$ )

\[ p \times{}_{S^{1}}S^{2 l + 1}\cong P_{l}( C ) . \]

Thus the critical manifolds corresponding to $p$ and $q$, are the sections $S_{p}$ and $S_{q}$ in $M_{l}$ indicated above. Hence,

since

\[ P_{t}\left(P_{l}( C )\right) = 1 + t^{2}+ \ldots + t^{2 l}\]

and the index of $f$ on $S^{2}$ is 0 at $p$ and 2 at $q$, we obtain the formula

\[ M_{t}\left(f_{l}\right) = \left(1 + t^{2}\right) \left(1 + t^{2}+ \ldots + t^{2 l}\right) \]

for the Morse series of the function $f_{l}$ induced by $f$ on $S^{2 l + 1}$.

When $l \rightarrow \infty$, this polynomial therefore becomes the formal power series

\[ M_{t}\left(f_{S^{1}}\right) = \left(1 + t^{2}\right) / \left(1 - t^{2}\right) . \]

Now cohomologically, the fibring $M_{l}$ over $P_{l}( C )$ is trivial (2-sphere bundles always are). Hence

by the Künneth formula

\[ P_{t}\left(M_{l}\right) = \left(1 + t^{2}\right) \left(1 + t^{2}+ \ldots + t^{2 l}\right) \]

and so we see by inspection that $\operatorname{our}f_{l}$ was indeed perfect, in fact not only on $M_{S^{1}}$ but also in each

approximation $M_{i}$.

Let us next modify $f$ so that it has a maximum at $p$ and at $q$, and a minimum along the equator,

but still keeping it $G = S^{1}$-invariant. Then, $p$ and $q$ both contribute

\[ t^{2}\left(1 + t^{2}+ \ldots + t^{2 l}\right) \]

to $M_{t}\left(f_{l}\right)$. On the other hand the critical set in $M_{l}$ corresponding to the equator on $S^{2}$, is given by

\[ S^{1}\times{}_{S^{1}}S^{2 l + 1}\simeq S^{2 l + 1}. \]

Hence it contributes $1 + t^{2 l + 1}$. Thus

\[ M_{t}\left(f_{l}\right) = \left(2 t^{2}\right) \left(1 + t^{2}+ \ldots + t^{2 l}\right) + \left(1 + t^{2 l + 1}\right) . \]

These functions are therefore not perfect for any particular $l$. On the other hand, letting $l \rightarrow \infty$,

we obtain

\[ \begin{aligned}M_{t}\left(f_{S^{1}}\right) & = 1 + 2 t^{2}+ 2 t^{4}+ \ldots \\ & = \left(1 + t^{2}\right) / \left(1 - t^{2}\right) , \end{aligned}\]

so that this new $f$ is again perfect according to our definition, i.e. on $M_{S_{1}}$.

This example illustrates two phenomena. First of all that a perfect $f_{G}$ on $M_{G}$ need not come

from a perfect $f$ on $M$. It also shows that in some sense the larger the orbit of a critical set of $f$

First of all recall the identity

\[ E / H \simeq E \times_{G}G / H , \]

when $E$ is a principal $G$-bundle and $H$ a closed subgroup of $G$. From this it follows immediately

that if $N \subset M$ is the $G$-orbit $G / H$, then

\[ E \times_{G}N \simeq E \times_{G}G / H \simeq E / H . \]

But a universal $G$-bundle $E$ is obviously also a universal $H$-bundle. Hence in the universal case

$E / H$ has the homotopy type of $B H$, the classifying space of $H$.

Hence by proposition 1.5 we have the following

Counting principle. The non-degenerate critical orbit $N = G / H$, of index $\lambda ( N )$ for fon $M$, contributes

(1.7)

\[ t^{\lambda ( N )}P_{t}( B H ) \]
