To analyse the implications of this relation further, recall the diagram

[?region]

which gave rise to the decomposition over $Q$,

\[ H^{*}( N ) \simeq H^{*}\left(N_{0}\right) \otimes H^{*}( J ) , \]

of proposition 9.7. Note in particular that $\eta^{*}$ is an isomorphism over $Q$. It follows that if we

introduce the new rational classes

[?line conf=0.80: \[ \begin{aligned}b_{2}^{j}= \pi^{*}\left(\eta^{*}\right)^{- 1}i^{*}b_{2}^{j}, & j = 1 , \ldots , 2 g , \\ a_{k}= \pi^{*}\left(\eta^{*}\right)^{- 1}i^{*}a_{k}, & k = 1,2 , \end{aligned}\]]

then these will generate $\pi^{*}H^{*}\left(N / J_{0}\right)$ so that as a $\Lambda^{*}\left(b_{1}^{j}\right)$-module, $H^{*}( N )$ is freely generated by

their span.

In terms of these variables, and the corresponding $\Lambda$, our polynomial $Q_{g}( a , \Lambda )$ is now trans-

formed into a polynomial $R_{g}( \boldsymbol{a}; \boldsymbol{\Lambda})$ and if this expression is expanded in terms of the basis

\[ b_{1}^{I}= b_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots b_{1}^{i_{k}}, \quad i_{1}< i_{2}< \ldots < i_{k}\]

for $\Lambda^{*}\left(b_{1}^{j}\right)$, then $R_{g}( \boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = 0$ implies that each coefficient in this expansion must vanish. Equi-

valently one can multiply $R_{g}$ by $b_{1}^{I}$ and integrate over the fibre of $\pi$, to obtain

\[ \pi_{*}b_{1}^{I}R_{g}( a ; \Lambda ) = 0 , \]

yielding a large number of relations in $a_{1}, a_{2}$ and $b_{2}^{i}$.

To carry out this process one first of all has to determine the old generators in terms of the

new bold-faced ones, and this is done quite easily by observing that $\operatorname{End}V$ descends to $N / J_{0}$

[?line conf=0.53: so that the characteristic classes of $\operatorname{End}V$ certainly are in the image of $\pi^{*}$. Thus $a_{1}+ \Lambda_{11}$ is in]

the image of $\pi^{*}$ and restricts to $i^{*}a_{1}$ on $N_{0}$ whence

[?line conf=0.84: \[ a_{1}= a_{1}+ \Lambda_{1}\quad \text{or}\quad a_{1}= a_{1}- \Lambda_{11}. \]]

Similarly $p_{1}( T )$ is in this image. Hence

\[ \pi^{*}\left(\eta^{*}\right)^{- 1}i^{*}\left(a_{1}^{2}- 4 a_{2}\right) = a_{1}^{2}- 4 a_{2}. \]

On the other hand as $i^{*}, \eta^{*}$ and $\pi^{*}$ are ring-homomorphisms the expression on the left is also

[?line conf=0.62: equal to $\boldsymbol{a}_{1}^{2}- \mathbf{4}\boldsymbol{a}_{2}$. Eliminating one obtains:]

\[ a_{2}= a_{2}- \frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{11}a_{1}+ \frac{1}{4}\Lambda_{11}^{2}. \]

[?line conf=0.56: Finally to determine $\boldsymbol{b}_{\mathbf{2}}^{i}$ consider $c_{\mathbf{2}}( \operatorname{End}V ) = c_{\mathbf{1}}^{2}- \mathbf{4}c_{\mathbf{2}}$, whose Künneth components must again]

all be in the image of $\pi^{*}$. Applied to the first mixed component this yields the result that

\[ \left(2 a , b_{1}^{i}- 4 b_{2}^{i}\right) \]

is in the image of $\pi^{*}$, whence

[?line conf=0.95: \[ b_{2}^{i}= b_{2}^{i}+ \frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}- \Lambda_{11}\right) b_{1}^{i}. \]]

Let us now expand our first relation $R_{g}( \boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda})$ in terms of $\Lambda_{11}$ and $\Lambda_{12}$, when $g = \mathbf{3}$. Also, in

part to avoid subscripts and in part to come closer to Ramanan's notation, let us set

\[ h = a_{1}, \quad \nu = a_{2}, \quad \theta = \Lambda_{22}\]
