Finally we revert to the geometry of the moduli space $N ( n , k )$ and show that it inherits a natural

Kähler structure. The essential observation is that the space $\mathscr{A}$ of unitary connections has a

[?line conf=0.69: natural symplectic structure: if $\alpha , \beta$ are two ad $( P )$-valued 1-forms on $M$ they have a skew product]

$\int_{M}\alpha \wedge \beta$ (we recall that this uses the inner product in the Lie algebra). This symplectic structure

is preserved by the action of $\mathscr{G}$. Moreover the curvature

\[ F : \mathscr{A}\rightarrow \Omega^{2}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) ) \]

can be identified with the corresponding moment map. To see this we first note that $\Omega^{2}( M , \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$

is canonically dual to $\Omega^{0}( M , \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$, which is the Lie algebra of $\mathscr{G}$. Hence for any $\phi \in \Omega^{0}( M , \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$

we have a real-valued function $F_{\phi}$ on $\mathscr{A}$, defined by $F_{\phi}( A ) = \left(F_{A}, \phi\right)$. To say that $F$ is the moment

map for the $\mathscr{G}$-action on $\mathscr{A}$ means that the Hamiltonian vector field on $\mathscr{A}$ defined by $F_{\phi}$ coincides

with the vector field given by the Lie algebra action of $\phi$. Equivalently we have to show that,

for any $\psi \in \Omega^{1}( M , \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$,

(9.14)

[?line conf=0.75: \[ \left(\mathrm{d}F_{\phi}, \psi\right) = \int \left(\mathrm{d}_{A}\phi\right) \wedge \psi . \]]

But, as we saw in §3, the left-hand side is equal to $\int \left(\mathrm{d}_{A}\psi\right) \wedge \phi$. Since $\mathrm{d}_{A}$ is a derivation and

$\int \mathrm{d}( \psi \wedge \phi ) = 0$ we have the usual formula for integration by parts

\[ \int \left(\mathrm{d}_{A}\psi\right) \wedge \phi = - \int \psi \wedge \left(\mathrm{d}_{A}\phi\right) \]

which verifies (9.14).

The constant central $U ( 1 )$ subgroup of $\mathscr{G}$ acts trivially on $\mathscr{A}$ corresponding to the fact that the

[?line conf=0.93: function $\int \operatorname{trace}F_{A}$ is constant and equal to $- 2 \pi \mathrm{i}k$ (where $k$ is the Chern class).]

The moment map is $\mathscr{G}$-equivariant and so to every orbit $C \subset \Omega^{2}( M , \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$ the inverse image

$F^{- 1}( C ) \subset \mathscr{A}$ is $\mathscr{G}$-invariant. The quotient $F^{- 1}( C ) / \mathscr{G}$ is sometimes called the Marsden-Weinstein

quotient. Under appropriate non-degeneracy conditions, it is a manifold and it inherits a natural

symplectic structure from that of $\mathscr{A}$. In particular taking $C$ to be the orbit given by the Yang-

Mills minimum (i.e. the constant conjugacy class with all eigenvalues $- 2 \pi \mathrm{i}k / n$ ) we obtain the

moduli space $N ( n , k )$. Thus $N ( n , k )$ inherits a natural symplectic structure.

The symplectic structure on $\mathscr{A}$ together with its natural metric defines the complex structure

of $\mathscr{C}$. Similarly the induced symplectic structure and metric on $N$ define its complex structure.

Thus $N$ is a Kähler manifold.

Note that the tangent space to $N$ at $E$ is $H^{1}( M , \operatorname{End}E )$ and it is easy to define the metric,

complex structure and symplectic structure on this tangent space. What is not immediately clear

is the global integrability condition of the complex and symplectic structures so defined on $N$.
