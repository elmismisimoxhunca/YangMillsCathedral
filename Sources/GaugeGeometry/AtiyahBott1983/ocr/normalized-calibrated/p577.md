[?line conf=0.61: so that $\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}= \mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{ss}}$ and Aut $E$ acts on $\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}( E )$ with only the constant central scalars as isotropy group]

action. It is a compact complex manifold: it even inherits a natural Kähler structure as we shall

see later. We want now to deduce what we can about the cohomology of $N ( n , k )$ from our general

results about equivariant cohomology.

Let us denote by $\overline{\mathscr{G}}$ the quotient of $\mathscr{G}$ by its constant central $U ( 1 )$-subgroup, and similarly

$\overline{\mathscr{G}}^{\mathrm{c}}$ will be the quotient of $\mathscr{G}\mathrm{c}= \operatorname{Aut}( E )$ by $C^{*}$. Thus $\overline{\mathscr{G}}^{\mathrm{c}}$ acts freely on $\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}$ with $N ( n , k )$ as quotient.

where on the right we have replaced $\overline{\mathscr{G}}^{\mathrm{c}}$ by $\overline{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{since}}$ they give the same cohomology. It remains to

(9.2)

[?line conf=0.76: \[ B U ( 1 ) \rightarrow B \mathscr{G}\rightarrow B \mathscr{G}, \]]

which is always trivial in rational cohomology. This is because restriction to a point of $M$

followed by taking determinants defines a homomorphism $\mathscr{G}\rightarrow U ( 1 )$ and the composition

$U ( 1 ) \rightarrow \mathscr{G}\rightarrow U ( 1 )$ is of degree $n$. This implies that

\[ H^{*}( B \mathscr{G}, Q ) \rightarrow H^{*}( B U ( 1 ) , Q ) \]

is surjective, which gives the triviality of the fibration over $Q$. Hence for any $\overline{\mathscr{G}}$-space $X$ the

$\mathscr{G}$-Poincaré series of $X$ is the product of the $\overline{\mathscr{G}}$-Poincaré series of $X$ and $\left(1 - t^{2}\right)^{- 1}$. Together with

(9.1) this then gives the formula for the Poincaré series of $N ( n , k )$ :

(9.3)

\[ P_{t}( N ( n , k ) ) = \left(1 - t^{2}\right) \mathscr{G}P_{t}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}\right) \]

where $\mathscr{G}P_{t}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}\right)$ is given inductively by theorem 7.14. As noted in the Introduction, and will be

elaborated in §11, this formula coincides with that of Desale \& Ramanan (1975), which rests on

the Harder-Narasimhan approach.
