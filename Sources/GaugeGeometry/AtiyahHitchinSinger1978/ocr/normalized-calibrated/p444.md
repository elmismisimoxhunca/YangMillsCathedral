1. First assume the space is non-empty, and that we have at least one self-dual

connection $\omega$. If $\omega'$ is another connection, the two differ by an element $\tau \in A^{1}( \mathrm{~g})$

and the relation between the two curvatures is

\[ \Omega'- \Omega = \mathrm{D}_{1}\tau + \frac{1}{2}[ \tau , \tau ] , \]

where $\mathrm{D}_{1}: A^{1}( \mathrm{~g}) \rightarrow A^{2}( \mathrm{~g})$ is the extended covariant derivative (2.1). Thus if $\omega_{t}$ is a

1 -parameter family of self-dual connections,

[?line conf=0.56: \[ \Omega_{t}= \Omega + \mathrm{D}_{1}\tau_{t}+ \frac{1}{2}\left[\tau_{t}, \tau_{t}\right] , \]]

and

\[ p_{-}\left(\mathrm{D}_{1}\tau_{t}+ \frac{1}{2}\left[\tau_{t}, \tau_{t}\right]\right) = 0 \in A_{-}^{2}( \mathrm{~g}) , \]

(6.2)

where $p_{-}$is the projection onto $\Lambda_{-}^{2}$, i.e.

\[ p_{-}\alpha = \frac{1}{2}( \alpha - * \alpha ) . \]

Differentiating with respect to $t$ and putting $t = 0$, we get $p_{-}\left(\mathrm{D}_{1}\dot{\tau}\right) = 0 \in A_{-}^{2}( \mathrm{~g})$

[?line conf=0.52: where $\dot{\tau}= \left. \left(\partial \tau_{t}/ \partial t\right)\right|_{t = 0}$. If the family were obtained by a 1-parameter family of]

[?line conf=0.58: gauge transformations, i.e. $\omega_{t}= f_{t}^{- 1}. \omega$ where $f_{t}\in \mathscr{G}$, then from § 2 we would have]

[?line conf=0.76: $\dot{\tau}= \nabla \dot{f}$ where $\dot{f}\in \Gamma ( \mathrm{~g}) = A^{0}( \mathrm{~g})$. Thus a 1-parameter family of self-dual connections]

[?line conf=0.49: defines an element in ker $p_{-}\mathrm{D}_{1}/ \operatorname{Im}\nabla$. Now $p_{-}\mathrm{D}_{1}\nabla = p_{-}\left(\mathrm{D}_{1}\nabla\right) = p_{-}( \Omega ) = 0$ since]

the connection is self-dual, so we have defined an element in the first cohomology

group $H^{1}( \mathrm{~g})$ of the following complex:

\[ 0 \rightarrow A^{0}( \mathrm{~g}) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{d}_{0}}A^{1}( \mathrm{~g}) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{d}_{1}}A_{-}^{2}( \mathrm{~g}) \rightarrow 0 , \]

[?line conf=0.56: where $\mathrm{d}_{0}= \nabla$ and $\mathrm{d}_{1}= p \_\mathrm{D}_{1}$. It is easy to see that this complex is elliptic and]

hence the cohomology groups are finite dimensional. The aim is to compute

$h^{1}= \operatorname{dim}H^{1}( \mathrm{~g})$ by calculating the alternating sum $h^{0}- h^{1}+ h^{2}$ by the index formula

and then using a vanishing theorem to show that $h^{0}= h^{2}= 0$.

[?line conf=0.94: In fact $h^{0}= \operatorname{dim}H^{0}( \mathrm{~g})$ and these are the covariant constant sections of $\mathfrak{g}$ which]

correspond to the Lie algebra of the centralizer of the holonomy group. Since by

hypothesis the connection is irreducible, this is the centre of $G$, which, since $G$ is

semi-simple, is zero-dimensional. Hence $h^{0}= 0$.

To proceed with the vanishing of $h^{2}$, it is useful to replace, in the standard way,

the above elliptic complex by a single elliptic operator

\[ \mathrm{d}+ \mathrm{d}^{*}: A^{1}( \mathrm{~g}) \rightarrow A^{0}( \mathrm{~g}) \oplus A^{2}( \mathrm{~g}) , \]

[?line conf=0.69: where $\mathrm{d}^{*}$ denotes the formal adjoint of $\mathrm{d}_{0}= \nabla$ by means of the Riemannian metric]
