It remains to examine the topological relation between the bundles $P_{X}$ and $P$. The inclusion

$G_{X}\subset G$ sends $S_{X}$ into $S$ (since $S$ is maximal semi-simple) and hence $G_{X}/ S_{X}\rightarrow G / S$. Also for the

universal coverings $\widetilde{S}_{X}$ is a factor of $\widetilde{S}$ and so the centre of $\widetilde{S}_{X}$ is a subgroup of the centre of $S$.

Elements of $\pi_{1}( G )$ can be viewed as pairs $( a , b )$ with

\[ a \in \pi_{1}( G / S ) , \quad b \in \text{centre}S . \]

Similarly elements of $\pi_{1}\left(G_{X}\right)$ are given by pairs $a_{X}, b_{X}$ and the homomorphism

\[ \pi_{1}\left(G_{X}\right) \rightarrow \pi_{1}( G ) \]

\[ \left\{\begin{array}{l}a_{X}\rightarrow a \\ a_{X}\equiv b_{X}\bmod D_{X}. \end{array}\right. \]

To sum up we see therefore that, for a given $C^{\infty}G$-bundle $P$ the Yang-Mills connections fall

into a countable number of families or types determined by conjugacy classes of elements $X$ in $\mathfrak{g}$.

These $X$ are constrained by the condition (6.17) and (6.18) rewritten in the form

(6.19)

\[ \left\{\begin{array}{l}X \rightarrow a \\ X \equiv b \bmod D_{X}\end{array}\right. \]

[?line conf=0.93: where $( a , b )$ are the pair determining the topology of $P$ with $a \in \pi_{1}( G / S ) , b \in$ centre $\tilde{S}$.]

In theorem 6.7 we formulated the results for full equivalence classes. If instead we pick a

base point $x_{0}\in M$ and work with the subgroup $\mathscr{G}_{0}$ of gauge transformations that are the identity

at $x_{0}$, then (6.7) becomes the statement that we have a bijection

\[ \operatorname{Hom}\left(\Gamma_{R}, G\right) \rightarrow \mathscr{M}/ \mathscr{G}_{0}\]

where $\mathscr{M}$ is the space of all Yang-Mills connections. The group $G = \mathscr{G}/ \mathscr{G}_{0}$ acts on both sides

and induces the bijection on quotients expressed in (6.7).

We shall now spell this out in more detail for the case of $G = U ( n )$. The Lie algebra is then the

space of skew-hermitian matrices. We write such a matrix as $- 2 \pi \mathrm{i}\Lambda$ so that $\Lambda$ is hermitian. Its

conjugacy class is determined by the eigenvalues $\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \ldots , \lambda_{n}$ of $\Lambda$, which we may arrange in

descending order:

(6.20)

\[ \lambda_{1}\geqslant \lambda_{2}\geqslant \ldots \geqslant \lambda_{n}. \]
