12. Convexity and Lie groups

This section is essentially an appendix concerned with the partial ordering that we have

encountered in our stratification of the space $\mathscr{C}$. We shall take this opportunity of giving a brief

but essentially self-contained account, which emphasizes the role of convexity in Lie groups.

The results are not essentially new, and can mainly be found in Horn (1954) for the unitary case

and in Kostant (1973) for the general groups, but our presentation brings out those aspects that

are of particular relevance to the theory of bundles and connections. In particular we stress the

role of convex invariant functions on the Lie algebra. For an extensive account of some aspects

see also Marshall \& Olkin (1979).

[?line conf=0.90: For simplicity we shall begin with the partial ordering (7.7) for sequences $\left(\lambda_{1}, \ldots , \lambda_{n}\right)$ of real]

numbers. Thus one defines $\mu \leqslant \lambda$ if, after arranging each sequence in decreasing order, we have

(12.1)

\[ \left\{\begin{array}{l}\sum_{j = 1}^{i}\mu_{j}\leqslant \sum_{j = 1}^{i}\lambda_{j}\quad \text{for}\quad i = 1 , \ldots , n - 1 , \\ \sum_{j = 1}^{n}\mu_{j}= \sum_{j = 1}^{n}\lambda_{j}. \end{array}\right. \]

This partial ordering occurs in Horn (1954) where it is shown to be equivalent to either of the

following properties:

(12.2)

(12.3)

\[ \begin{gathered}\sum_{j}f \left(\mu_{j}\right) \leqslant \sum_{j}f \left(\lambda_{j}\right) \quad \text{for every convex function}f : R \rightarrow R ; \\ \mu = P \lambda \text{where}\lambda , \mu \in R^{n}\text{and}P \text{is a doubly stochastic matrix.}\end{gathered}\]

[?line conf=0.83: We recall that a real square matrix $P = \left(p_{i j}\right)$ is stochastic if $p_{i j}\geqslant 0$ and $\sum_{j}p_{i j}= 1$ for all $i$. If in]

addition the transposed matrix is also stochastic then $P$ is called doubly stochastic. A theorem of

G. D. Birkhoff identifies doubly stochastic matrices in terms of permutation matrices, namely

The doubly stochastic $n \times n$ matrices are the convex hull of the permutation matrices.

In view of this (12.3) can be replaced by

(12.4)

\[ \widehat{\Sigma_{n}\mu}\subset \widehat{\Sigma_{n}\lambda}\]

where $\Sigma_{n}x$ denotes the orbit of any $x \in R^{n}$ under the permutation group $\Sigma_{n}$, and $\hat{C}$ denotes the

convex hull of the set $C \subset R^{n}$.

Geometric notions of convexity can be dualized into statements about convex functions by

virtue of the fact that, for $C \subset R^{n}$,

[?line conf=0.76: \[ x \in \hat{C}\Leftrightarrow \phi ( x ) \leqslant \sup_{C}\phi \quad \text{for all convex}\quad \phi : R^{n}\rightarrow R . \]]

[?line conf=0.94: Thus taking $\phi$ to be a convex symmetric function on $R^{n}$ (i.e. invariant under $\Sigma_{n}$ ) we can see that]

(12.4) implies

(12.5)

\[ \phi ( \mu ) \leqslant \phi ( \lambda ) \text{for all convex symmetric functions on}R^{n}\text{.}\]

Since (12.2) is the special case of (12.5) for functions $\phi \left(x_{1}, \ldots , x_{n}\right)$ of the form $\sum_{i = 1}^{n}f \left(x_{i}\right)$, it follows

that (12.5) implies (12.2) and so is equivalent to all the other properties.

Schur showed that if $\mu_{j}( j = 1 , \ldots , n )$ are the diagonal elements of a hermitian matrix whose

eigenvalues are $\lambda_{j}$, then $\mu \leqslant \lambda$ in the sense of (12.1). Horn (1954) proved the converse so that

another equivalent of (12.1) is

(12.6)

\[ \text{the}\lambda_{j}\text{are the eigenvalues of a hermitian matrix with diagonal elements}\mu_{j}\text{.}\]
