M. F. ATIYAH AND R. BOTT

and at this stage we are ready to compute $c_{1}( T )$. Recall first that for any $n$-dimensional bundle $V$

\[ c_{2}( \operatorname{End}V ) = ( n - 1 ) c_{1}^{2}( V ) - 2 n c_{2}( V ) . \]

Hence

[?line conf=0.90: \[ \begin{aligned}c_{1}\left(f_{!}\operatorname{End}V\right) & = \operatorname{ch}_{1}\left(f_{!}\operatorname{End}V\right) \\ & = - f_{*}\left\{( n - 1 ) c_{1}^{2}- 2 n c_{2}\right\}\\ & \left. = - \left\{2 ( n - 1 ) a_{1}f_{1}+ \Lambda_{11}\right) - 2 n f_{2}\right\}\\ & = - 2 \left(a_{1}+ \Lambda_{11}\right) , \end{aligned}\]]

so that

\[ c_{1}( T ) = 2 \left(a_{1}+ \Lambda_{11}\right) . \]

The first part of our proposition now follows by restriction to $N_{0}$. The second part also follows

because $a_{1}$ must generate $H^{2}\left(N_{0}, Z\right)$ by theorem 9.11. Note also that $\operatorname{dim}H^{2}\left(N_{0}\right) = 1$ from the

Poincaré series for $N_{0}$.

Before proceeding to a more detailed account of the case $n = 2$, observe that if we define the

total Pontryagin class $p ( T )$ of $T$ as the product of the total Chern classes of $T$ and $T^{*}$,

\[ p ( T ) = c ( T ) c \left(T^{*}\right) , \]

then our relation for $T + T^{*}$ in $K ( N )$ implies the formula

\[ p ( T ) = c \left(\operatorname{End}\mid N_{0}\right)^{2 \bar{g}}. \]

[?line conf=0.73: This follows from the Riemann-Roch theorem, or also from the fact that the support of $\Omega^{1}- 1$]

is at a point of $M$ and $c_{1}\left(\Omega^{1}- 1\right) = 2 \bar{g}$. The formula is especially simple in the rank 2 case where

(cf. Newstead 1972)

\[ c ( \operatorname{End}V \mid N ) = 1 + \left(a_{1}^{2}- 4 a_{2}\right) . \]

Thus the ring Pont $( T )$ generated by all the Pontryagin classes of $T$ is actually generated by the

single element $\left(a_{1}^{2}+ 4 a_{2}\right) = - p_{1}$.

Newstead made two conjectures about the Pontryagin and Chern classes of $T$ : first of all that

$c_{i}( T ) = 0$ for $i > 2 \bar{g}$, and secondly that $\operatorname{Pont}( T ) = 0$ in $\operatorname{dim}> 4 \bar{g}$. The first of these conjectures

has been recently proved by Gieseker (1982). The second conjecture remains open but, in view

of our formula for $p ( T )$, is equivalent to the assertion that $p_{1}^{g}= 0$.

We turn now finally to a more detailed examination of the relations we are after in the rank 2

case. Here we have to deal with only two Chern classes for $V$ and they are given by

[?line conf=0.78: \[ \begin{aligned}& c_{1}= a_{1}+ \xi_{1}+ k \omega , \quad k = 2 n \bar{g}+ 1 \\ & c_{2}= a_{2}+ \xi_{2}+ \left\{( 2 \bar{g}+ 1 ) a_{1}+ \Lambda_{11}\right\}\omega , \end{aligned}\]]

where we have substituted for $f_{2}$ the expression already found earlier. Applying the Riemann-

Roch procedure one therefore obtains universal polynomials $Q_{q}( a ; \Lambda )$ in $\left(a_{1}, a_{2}, \Lambda_{11}, \Lambda_{12}, \Lambda_{22}\right)$

such that

[?line conf=0.89: \[ c_{q}\left(f_{!}V\right) = Q_{q}( a ; \Lambda ) . \]]

Hence the relations take the form

[?line conf=0.60: \[ Q_{q}( a ; \Lambda ) = 0 \quad \text{for}\quad q \geqslant 2 g \text{,}\]]

so that the 'first' of these asserts that

\[ Q_{2 g}( a ; \Lambda ) = 0 . \]
