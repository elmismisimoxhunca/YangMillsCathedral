Lemma 12.14. If $x , y \in C$ then

\[ y \in \widehat{W}x \Leftrightarrow y \leqslant x . \]

Proof. Let us first illustrate the geometric meaning of this for $\operatorname{SU}( 3 )$, in which case the diagram

is as below.

Figure 4.

The shaded region is the intersection $C$ with the 'backward' $C^{*}$-cone centred at $x$. It therefore

describes the set $y$ such that

\[ y \in C \quad \text{and}\quad y \leqslant x . \]

The lemma asserts that this set is also the intersection of $C$ and the hexagon $\widehat{W x}$. In one direction

this is easy because for $y \in \widehat{W}x$ we have

\[ y = \sum_{\omega \in W}a_{\omega}\omega x , \quad a_{\omega}\geqslant 0 , \quad \sum a_{\omega}= 1 \]

so that

\[ x = \left(\sum a_{\omega}\right) x \geqslant \sum a_{\omega}\omega x = y \]

by (12.12). For the converse it will be enough by continuity to assume that $x$ is an interior point of

$C$ and that $x - y$ is an interior point of $C^{*}$. The directed line $x y$ then meets the boundary of $C$ in a

point $z$ and we must show that the whole finite interval of $x z$ lies in $\widehat{W x}$. Since the relation $y \in \widehat{W x}$

is transitive, it will be enough to show that there is a constant $c ( x , z )$ so that, if $y = t z + ( 1 - t ) x$,

with $0 \leqslant t \leqslant 1$, is any point in the interval $z x$, then

(i) $c ( y , z ) \geqslant c ( x , z )$,

(ii) $y \in \widehat{W x}$ if $t \leqslant c ( x , z )$.
