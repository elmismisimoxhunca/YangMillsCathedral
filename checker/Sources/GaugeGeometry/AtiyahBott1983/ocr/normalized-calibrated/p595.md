Finally therefore (11.6) gives an explicit inductive formula for $\beta ( n , k )$ in terms of $\beta \left(n_{i}, k_{i}\right)$ with

$n_{i}< n$. The formula is given rationally in $q$ and the $\omega_{i}$ and is independent of the line-bundle $L$

(Desale \& Ramanan 1975; proposition 1.7). To get the Poincaré polynomial $P_{t}\left(N_{0}( n , k )\right)$ one

now makes the substitution

\[ \omega_{i}\rightarrow - t , q \rightarrow t^{2}. \]

For the purposes of comparison with our method let us now examine in detail the case $n = 2$

and $k = 1$. In (11.6) we then have stable bundles, which contribute (11.7), and unstable bundles,

which have a canonical filtration

(11.10)

\[ 0 \rightarrow L_{1}\rightarrow E \rightarrow L_{2}\rightarrow 0 \]

where $\operatorname{deg}L_{1}= r + 1 , \operatorname{deg}L_{2}= - r$ for $r = 0,1 , \ldots$ and $L_{1}\otimes L_{2}\cong L$. To compute $| \operatorname{Aut}( E ) |$

consider separately the trivial and non-trivial extensions. For $E = L_{1}\oplus L_{2}$ the automorphisms

consist of $F_{q}^{*}\times F_{q}^{*}$ together with the unipotents of the form $1 + \phi$ with

\[ \phi \in \operatorname{Hom}\left(L_{2}, L_{1}\right) = H^{0}\left(M , L_{2}^{*}\otimes L_{1}\right) . \]

Hence

\[ | \operatorname{Aut}( E ) | = ( q - 1 )^{2}h_{0}, \]

where $h_{0}= \left|H^{0}\left(M , L_{2}^{*}\otimes L_{1}\right)\right|$. On the other hand for non-trivial extensions we have only one

copy of $F_{q}^{*}$ and so

\[ | \operatorname{Aut}( E ) | = ( q - 1 ) h_{0}. \]

The non-trivial extensions correspond to non-zero elements of $H^{1}\left(M , L_{2}^{*}\otimes L_{1}\right)$ and proportional

vectors give isomorphic bundles. Hence the number of isomorphism classes of bundles $E$ for

which (11.10) is non-trivial is

\[ \left(h_{1}- 1\right) / ( q - 1 ) , \]

where

\[ h_{1}= \left|H^{1}\left(M , L_{2}^{*}\otimes L_{1}\right)\right| . \]

Hence the contribution to the sum in (11.6) arising from a given $L_{1}$ ( $L_{2}$ being then determined

as $\left. L \otimes L_{1}^{*}\right)$ is

\[ \frac{1}{( q - 1 )^{2}h_{0}}+ \frac{h_{1}- 1}{( q - 1 )^{2}h_{0}}= \frac{h_{1}}{( q - 1 )^{2}h_{0}}. \]

Now by Riemann-Roch we have

\[ \operatorname{dim}H^{0}\left(M , L_{2}^{*}\otimes L_{1}\right) - \operatorname{dim}H^{1}\left(M , L_{2}^{*}\otimes L_{1}\right) = 2 r + 2 - g , \]

and so

\[ h_{0}/ h_{1}= q^{2 r + 2 - g}. \]

Thus (11.6) becomes

(11.11)

\[ \frac{\left|N_{L}( 2,1 )\right|}{q - 1}+ \frac{J_{q}}{( q - 1 )^{2}}\sum_{r = 0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{2 r + 2 - g}}= \frac{1}{q - 1}q^{3 g - 3}\zeta_{M}( 2 ) . \]

Substituting for $\zeta_{M}( 2 )$ from (11.5) and for $J_{q}$ from (11.9) we get

(11.12)

\[ \left|N_{L}( 2,1 )\right| + \frac{q^{g - 2}\prod_{i = 1}^{2 g}\left(1 - \omega_{i}\right)}{( q - 1 ) \left(1 - q^{- 2}\right)}= \frac{q^{3 g - 3}\prod_{i = 1}^{2 g}\left(1 - \omega_{i}q^{- 2}\right)}{\left(1 - q^{- 2}\right) \left(1 - q^{- 1}\right)}. \]

If we now make the substitution $\omega_{i}\rightarrow - t , q \rightarrow t^{2}$ then the expression for $\left|N_{L}( 2,1 )\right|$ given by (11.12)

converts into the formula for the Poincaré polynomial given in (11.3).
