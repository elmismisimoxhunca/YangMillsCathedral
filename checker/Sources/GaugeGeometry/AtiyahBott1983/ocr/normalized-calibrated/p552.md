M. F. ATIYAH AND R. BOTT

With the help of our lemma, it is now an easy matter to compute the first and second variations

of $L$.

Proposition (4.6). The connection $A$ is stationary for $L ( A ) = \| F ( A ) \|^{2}$, if and only if

\[ \mathrm{d}_{A}* F ( A ) = 0 . \]

Proof. Expanding $F_{t}= F \left(A_{t}\right)$ according to (4.5) gives

(4.7)

\[ \left\| F_{t}\right\|^{2}= \| F \|^{2}+ 2 t \left(\mathrm{~d}_{A}\eta , F\right) + t^{2}\left\{\left\| \mathrm{~d}_{A}\eta \right\|^{2}+ ( F , [ \eta , \eta ] ) \right\}+ \text{higher terms}. \]

Hence at an extremum $\left(\mathrm{d}_{A}\eta , F\right) = 0$, or equivalently

\[ \left(\eta , \mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}F\right) = 0 , \]

for all $\eta \in \Omega^{1}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$. Hence at an extremum

(4.8)

\[ \mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}F ( A ) = 0 . \]

Here $\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}$ is the adjoint of $\mathrm{d}_{A}$ relative to our norm on $\Omega^{*}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$ and, just as in the usual

Hodge theory, it is given by $\pm * \mathrm{~d}_{A}*$. Precisely, if $m = \operatorname{dim}M$, then

[?line conf=0.52: (4.9)]

\[ \mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}= ( - 1 )^{m + m p + 1}* \mathrm{~d}_{A}* \quad \text{on}\quad \Omega^{p}. \]

Hence (4.9) implies (4.6).

For completeness we quickly review the proof of (4.9). Suppose then that $\varphi \in \Omega^{p - 1}, \psi \in \Omega^{p}$,

one of them having compact support. Then by (4.4)

\[ \mathrm{d}_{A}\{\varphi \wedge * \psi \}= \mathrm{d}_{A}\varphi \wedge * \psi + ( - 1 )^{p - 1}\varphi \wedge \mathrm{~d}_{A}* \psi . \]

Hence integration yields

\[ 0 = \left(\mathrm{d}_{A}\varphi , \psi\right) + ( - 1 )^{p - 1}\left(\varphi , *^{- 1}\mathrm{~d}_{A}* \psi\right) . \]

But

\[ *^{- 1}= ( - 1 )^{( p - 1 ) ( m - p + 1 )}* \]

on ( $\mathrm{p}- 1$ )-forms, yielding (4.9).

Remark. The Bianchi identities assert that for every $A$, we have $\mathrm{d}_{A}F ( A ) = 0$. Hence at a

stationary point we have both

\[ \mathrm{d}_{A}F ( A ) = 0 \quad \text{and}\quad \mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}F ( A ) = 0 . \]

Forms satisfying these two equations are clearly (nonlinear!) analogues of harmonic forms in the

usual Hodge theory. In short the condition for $A$ to be extremal is precisely that its curvature

$F ( A )$ be harmonic in $\Omega^{2}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$.

The expansion (4.7) of course also yields the Hessian of $L$ at an extremal connection $A$. This

Hessian is a quadratic form on the tangent space to $\mathscr{A}( P )$ at $A$, which in our identification is

precisely $\Omega^{1}( M ; \operatorname{ad}( P ) )$. With this understood we have the formula for the second variation.

Proposition (4.10). The quadratic form $Q ( \eta , \eta )$ defined by the Hessian of $L$ at an extremal connection

$A$ is given by

\[ Q ( \eta , \eta ) = \left(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}\mathrm{~d}_{A}\eta + * [ * F , \eta ] , \eta\right) . \]

Proof. From (4.7) we have

\[ Q ( \eta , \eta ) = \left. \frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{~d}^{2}}{\mathrm{~d}t^{2}}\left\| F_{t}\right\|^{2}\right|_{t = 0}= \left\| \mathrm{d}_{A}\eta \right\|^{2}+ ( F , [ \eta , \eta ] ) . \]

To bring this expression into the required form observe that $\left\| \mathrm{d}_{A}\eta \right\|^{2}= \left(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}\mathrm{~d}_{A}\eta , \eta\right)$, while

(4.11)

\[ ( F , [ \eta , \eta ] ) = \int_{M}[ \eta , \eta ] \wedge * F = \int_{M}\eta \wedge [ \eta , * F ] = ( - 1 )^{m + 1}\int_{M}\eta \wedge * *^{- 1}[ * F , \eta ] . \]

Using the formula for $*^{- 1}$ this reduces to $( \eta , * [ * F , \eta ] )$.
