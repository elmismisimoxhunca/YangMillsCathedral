YANG-MILLS EQUATIONS OVER RIEMANN SURFACES

Pulling back the universal vector bundle over $B U ( n )$ we then get a vector bundle $V$ over $B \mathscr{G}\times M$.

Since $M$ has no torsion the Künneth formula gives, for integer cohomology,

(2.17)

\[ H^{2 r}( B \mathscr{G}\times M ) \cong H^{2 r}( B \mathscr{G}) \oplus H^{2 r - 1}( B \mathscr{G}) \otimes H^{1}( M ) \oplus H^{2 r - 2}( B \mathscr{G}) \otimes H^{2}( M ) \]

Taking the Chern class $c_{r}( V )$ and decomposing it, we get from this Künneth decomposition,

elements

[?line conf=0.50: \[ \begin{aligned}& a_{r}\in H^{2 r}( B \mathscr{G}) \\ & b_{r}^{j}\in H^{2 r - 1}( B \mathscr{G}) , \quad j = 1 , \ldots , 2 g \\ & f_{r}\in H^{2 r - 2}( B \mathscr{G}) \end{aligned}\]]

relative to a basis $\alpha_{j}$ of $H^{1}( M )$. These will give rational generators, but to get integral generators

we need to replace the $f_{r}$ by elements $d_{r}$ constructed as follows. We introduce $K$-theory instead of

cohomology and analogous to (2.17) we have a Künneth formula

(2.18)

\[ K^{0}( B \mathscr{G}\times M ) \cong K^{1}( B \mathscr{G}) \otimes K^{1}( M ) \oplus K^{0}( B \mathscr{G}) \otimes K^{0}( M ) \]

Now we have

\[ K^{0}( M ) \cong Z \oplus Z \]

with two canonical generators, the first given by the trivial line-bundle and the second by the

reduced line-bundle of Chern class 1 (i.e. $H - 1$, where $H$ is the line-bundle and 1 the trivial

line-bundle). Starting with the class of $V$ in $K^{0}( B \mathscr{G}\times M )$ and projecting onto the second com-

ponent then gives an element $W \in K^{0}( B \mathscr{G})$. An alternative description of $W$ is to say that

[?line conf=0.50: \[ W = f_{1}( \tilde{V}) \]]

[?line conf=0.93: where $f : B \mathscr{G}\times M \rightarrow B \mathscr{G}$ is the projection, $f_{!}$is the direct image map in $K$-theory and $\tilde{V}= V - 1 \otimes V_{0}$]

with $V_{0}= V \mid B \mathscr{G}\times$ point. Since $f_{!}( 1 ) = 1 - g$ it follows that

(2.19)

\[ W = f_{!}( \tilde{V}) = f_{!}( V ) + ( g - 1 ) V_{0}. \]

Finally, taking the Chern classes of $W$ we get an infinite sequence of elements

\[ e_{r}\in H^{2 r}( B \mathscr{G}) , \quad r = 1,2 , \ldots . \]

We shall now prove

Proposition 2.20. The elements $a_{r}, b_{r}^{j}, e_{r}$ constructed above are multiplicative generators of the integral

cohomology ring of $B \mathscr{G}$. The elements $a_{r}, b_{r}^{j}, f_{r}$ are multiplicative generators of the rational cohomology ring.

Remark. The $a_{r}$ are the Chern classes of $V_{0}$ so that by (2.19) we get another set of generators by

replacing the $e_{r}$ by the Chern classes $d_{r}$ of $f_{!}( V )$. These will occur more naturally in algebraic

geometry.

The three types of element will in fact provide generators for the three factors in the fibration

decomposition (2.13) and (2.14). Clearly the $a_{r}$ give the Chern classes of $B U ( n )$ and so generate

its cohomology. The classes $b_{r}^{j}$ (for fixed $j$ ) are easily seen to give the generators for the cohomo-

logy of the $j$ th factor $U ( n )$ in $U ( n )^{2 g}$. It remains to show that the elements $e_{r}$ give generators for

the cohomology of $\Omega U ( n )$. Now we have a natural stabilization map

\[ i : \Omega U ( n ) \rightarrow \Omega U , \]

where $U = \lim_{m \rightarrow \infty}U ( n )$ is the stable unitary group. The periodicity theorem gives a homotopy

equivalence

\[ \Omega U \sim Z \times B U \]
