Because $\psi$ is $G$-invariant so is $\Gamma ( \psi )$ and it is therefore sufficient to prove the existence of a

supporting hyperplane to $\Gamma ( \psi )$ at boundary points $( \lambda , y )$ of $\Gamma ( \phi )$. By hypothesis $\Gamma ( \phi )$ is convex

so we have a supporting hyperplane $H \subset \mathfrak{t}\oplus R$. Let $H'= \pi^{- 1}( H ) \subset \mathfrak{g}\oplus R$ where $\pi$ is orthogonal

projection. We shall show that $H'$ is the required supporting hyperplane for $\Gamma ( \psi )$. Any point

$( x , y ) \in \Gamma ( \psi )$ satisfies $y > \psi ( x )$. From (12.15) and the convexity of $\phi$ we see that

\[ \psi ( x ) \geqslant \phi ( \pi x ) . \]

Hence $y > \phi ( \pi x )$ so that $( \pi x , y ) \in \Gamma ( \phi )$ and hence is on one side of $H$. This means precisely that

$( x , y )$ is on the corresponding side of $H'$, which completes the proof.

As with $U ( n )$ we can give several further equivalent definitions of the partial ordering and we

summarize this in

Proposition 12.17. The following conditions $x , y \in \mathfrak{t}$ are all equivalent:

(1)

\[ \widehat{W y}\subset \widehat{W x}; \]

(2)

[?line conf=0.64: \[ \phi ( y ) \leqslant \phi ( x ) \text{for all}W \text{-invariant convex functions}\phi \text{on}\mathfrak{t}\text{;}\]]

(3)

\[ \widehat{G y}\subset \widehat{G x}; \]

(4)

[?line conf=0.91: \[ \psi ( y ) \leqslant \psi ( x ) \text{for all}G \text{-invariant convex functions}\psi \text{on}\mathfrak{g}\text{.}\]]

[?line conf=0.28: Proof. (1) ⇒ (2), (2) ⇒ (4), (1) $= ( 3 )$ and (3) ⇒ (4) are all trivial. (3) ⇒ (1) follows from (12.15)]

and (4) ⇒ (2) follows from (12.16). It remains to see that (2) ⇒ (1). For this we take

\[ \phi ( x ) = \sum_{\omega \in W}\exp \left\langle \omega t x , e_{i}\right\rangle \quad ( t > 0 ) , \]

where the $e_{i}$ are a basis of $C$ (the 'edges' of the cone) and let $x , y \in C$. In view of (12.11) and

(12.12) we have

[?line conf=0.76: \[ \begin{aligned}& \left\langle x , e_{i}\right\rangle \geqslant \left\langle \omega x , e_{i}\right\rangle \quad \text{for}\quad \omega \neq 1 \quad \text{and all}\quad i , \\ & \left\langle y , e_{i}\right\rangle \geqslant \left\langle \omega y , e_{i}\right\rangle . \end{aligned}\]]

It will be sufficient by continuity to suppose that both $x$ and $y$ are interior to $C$; then the above

inequalities are strict so that for large $t$ the first term (for $\omega = 1$ ) in the sum defining $\phi$ is dominant.

Hence

[?line conf=0.58: \[ \begin{aligned}\phi ( y ) \leqslant \phi ( x ) & \Rightarrow \left\langle x , e_{i}\right\rangle \geqslant \left\langle y , e_{i}\right\rangle \text{for all}i \\ & \Rightarrow x - y \in C^{*}\\ & \Rightarrow y \leqslant x \text{by}\end{aligned}\]]

(12.11).

Remark. Proposition 12.17 remains true when 'convex' is interpreted as 'smooth convex'

(or even analytic). This is clear from the proof because, for the essential implication (2) ⇒ (1),

we use only exponential functions.

If we take any irreducible representation $\rho : G \rightarrow U ( n )$ it has weights $\lambda_{1}, \ldots , \lambda_{n}$, which we

[?line conf=0.94: may view as elements of $\mathfrak{t}$, so that any $x \in \mathfrak{t}$ gives rise to the hermitian matrix with eigenvalues]

$x_{j}= \left\langle x , \lambda_{j}\right\rangle$. If $\lambda_{1}$ is the maximal weight then $\lambda_{1}\in C$ and for all $j \neq 1 , \lambda_{1}> \lambda_{j}$, i.e. $\lambda_{1}- \lambda_{j}\in C^{*}$.

This means that, if $x \in C$, then $x_{1}\geqslant x_{j}$ for $j \neq 1$, so that $x_{1}$ is the largest eigenvalue. Hence if

[?line conf=0.84: $x , y \in C$ and we assume $p ( y ) \leqslant \rho ( x )$ then in particular $y_{1}\leqslant x_{1}$, i.e. $\left\langle y , \lambda_{1}\right\rangle \leqslant \left\langle x , \lambda_{1}\right\rangle$. If we let $\rho$]

run over all irreducible representations then $\lambda_{1}$ runs over all integral dominant weights and these

span $C$ : in fact there are $l$ basic integral weights $e_{i}$ that lie in the edges of $C$ and generate it. This

proves (cf. Kostant 1973)

[?line conf=0.80: Proposition 12.18. For $x , y \in \mathfrak{g}$ we have $y \leqslant x \Leftrightarrow \rho ( y ) \leqslant \rho ( x )$ for all unitary representations $\rho$.]
