14. Sobolev spaces

In this section we shall show, by introducing appropriate Sobolev spaces of functions, how to

justify our heuristic use of infinite-dimensional manifolds. Much of this is standard and can be

found in Narasimhan \& Ramadas (1979), Uhlenbeck (1981) or Mitter \& Viallet (1981) but some

of the more detailed results related to the complex structure depend of course on the dimension-

[?line conf=0.95: ality of the base manifold being 2 . For this reason we shall give a self-contained account tailored]

to our purposes.

For the convenience of the reader we shall now recall some of the basic facts about Sobolev

[?line conf=0.54: spaces. For fuller details we refer to Palais ( $1965 , \mathrm{ch}.9$ ). On a compact smooth $n$-dimensional]

manifold $M$ the space $L_{k}^{p}$ (for $1 \leqslant p < \infty$ ) denotes those functions $f$ all of whose derivatives up to

and including order $k$ are in the Lebesgue space $L^{p}$. The definition can be extended to non-

integral $k$ and to sections $f$ of any smooth complex vector bundle over $M$. Each $L_{k}^{p}$ is a Banach

space and for $p = 2$ is a Hilbert space also denoted by $H^{k}$. The Sobolev embedding theorems

assert that

(14.1)

[?line conf=0.71: \[ \begin{array}{r}L_{k}^{p}\subset L_{l}^{q}\text{if}k \geqslant l \text{and}k - n / p \geqslant l - n / q \text{and the inclusion is compact}\\ \text{if we have strict inequalities,}\end{array}\]]

(14.2)

[?line conf=0.58: \[ L_{k}^{p}\subset C^{l}\quad \text{if}k - n / p > l , \quad \text{and the inclusion is compact.}\]]

Here $C^{l}$ (for integral $l \geqslant 0$ ) denotes as usual sections whose partial derivatives of order $\leqslant l$ are

continuous. In particular

(14.3)

\[ \bigcap_{k = 1}^{\infty}H^{k}= C^{\infty}. \]
