that $* = - \mathrm{i}$ on $\Omega^{1,0}$ and $* = \mathrm{i}$ on $\Omega^{0,1}$.

For instance, for $\alpha \in \Omega^{0,0}$ we obtain

[?line conf=0.73: \[ \begin{aligned}\Delta_{A}\alpha & = - * \mathrm{~d}_{A}* \mathrm{~d}_{A}\alpha \\ & = + * \mathrm{~d}_{\left(\mathrm{id}_{A}'\alpha - \mathrm{id}_{A}^{\prime \prime}\alpha\right)}\\ & = \mathrm{i}* \left(\mathrm{~d}_{A}^{\prime \prime}\mathrm{d}_{A}'- \mathrm{d}_{A}'\mathrm{d}_{A}^{\prime \prime}\right) \alpha \\ & = \square_{A}'\alpha + \square_{A}^{\prime \prime}\alpha . \end{aligned}\]]

Thus the lower $\mathrm{d}_{A}^{\prime \prime}$, together with Hodge theory, yields

\[ \operatorname{index}Q = \operatorname{dim}H^{1}\left(M ; \operatorname{ad}_{- \lambda}( P )\right) \]

Finally if we take $\lambda < 0$ a completely analogous argument yields

\[ \operatorname{index}Q = \operatorname{dim}H^{1}\left(M ; \operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}( P )\right) . \]
