Taking determinants gives a natural map

(9.4)

\[ \operatorname{det}: N ( n , k ) \rightarrow J_{k}, \]

where $J_{k}$ is the Jacobian of $M$, parametrizing line-bundles of degree $k$. Clearly $J_{0}$ acts on $N ( n , k )$

by tensor product and the determinant becomes a $J_{0}$-equivariant map if we make $J_{0}$ act on $J_{k}$

via the $n$th power map

\[ \sigma_{n}: J_{0}\rightarrow J_{0}. \]

This shows that after lifting to a finite covering, with group $\operatorname{Ker}\sigma_{n}\cong H^{1}\left(M , Z_{n}\right) \cong \Gamma_{n}= \Gamma / n \Gamma$,

(9.4) becomes a product. Thus if we denote by $N_{0}( n , k )$ the fibre of (9.4) then

(9.5)

\[ N ( n , k ) = \left(N_{0}( n , k ) \times J_{k}\right) / \Gamma_{n}. \]

The manifold $N_{0}( n , k )$ is the moduli space of stable bundles with fixed determinant. If we now take

$\mathscr{G}'\subset \mathscr{G}$ corresponding to the lattice $n \Gamma \subset \Gamma$, so that $\mathscr{G}/ \mathscr{G}'= \Gamma / n \Gamma = \Gamma_{n}$ then the analogue of

(9.1) becomes

[?line conf=0.89: (9.6)]

\[ H^{*}\left(N_{0}( n , k ) \times J_{k}\right) \cong H_{\mathscr{G}}^{*}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}\right) . \]

Since $\mathscr{G}$ and $\mathscr{G}'$ give the same equivariant cohomology of $\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}$ (over $Q$ ) the same holds for $\overline{\mathscr{G}}$ and

$\overline{\mathscr{G}}'$, and so comparing (9.1) and (9.6) we get

Proposition 9.7. For rational cohomology we have

\[ H^{*}( N ( n , k ) ) \cong H^{*}\left(N_{0}( n , k )\right) \otimes H^{*}( J ) \]

or in terms of Poincaré polynomials

\[ P_{t}( N ( n , k ) ) = P_{t}\left(N_{0}( n , k )\right) ( 1 + t )^{2 g}. \]

This proposition, which is equivalent to saying that $\Gamma_{n}$ acts trivially on the rational cohomology

of $N_{0}( n , k )$, was the main result of Harder \& Narasimhan (1975) where it was proved by number-

theoretic methods comparing $G L ( n )$ with $S L ( n )$. For us the triviality of the action of $\Gamma_{n}$ is a

consequence of its triviality on the cohomology of $B \mathscr{G}'$.

We turn next to the integral cohomology of the moduli space $N ( n , k )$. We want to prove that

[?line conf=0.82: it has no torsion. We already know by (7.17) that $H_{\mathscr{G}}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}\right)$ has no torsion and, in view of (9.1),]

[?line conf=0.76: we want to deduce the same result for $H_{\overline{\mathscr{G}}}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}\right)$. It will be sufficient to prove that the fibration (9.2)]

is in fact a product so that

[?line conf=0.43: \[ H_{\mathscr{G}}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}\right) \cong H_{\overrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{s}}\right) \otimes H ( B U ( 1 ) ) . \]]

Now $B U ( 1 )$ is an Eilenberg-Maclane space $K ( Z , 2 )$ and so (9.2) has a characteristic class in

$H^{3}( B \overline{\mathscr{G}}, Z )$ whose vanishing will imply the triviality of the fibration. Equivalently we need to

show that

(9.8)

\[ H^{2}( B \mathscr{G}, Z ) \rightarrow H^{2}( B U ( 1 ) , Z ) \]

is surjective, but this was the content of proposition 2.21. Thus we have now proved

Theorem 9.9. If $( n , k ) = 1$ the moduli space $N ( n , k )$ of stable bundles has no torsion.
