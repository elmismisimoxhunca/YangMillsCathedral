Now let $\alpha_{i}$ be the simple roots normalized to have length one, so that the $\alpha_{i}$ are the unit normals

to the faces of $C$ and form the basis for $C^{*}$. Since $x$ is assumed interior to $C$ we have

[?line conf=0.92: \[ \left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle > 0 \text{for all}i . \]]

Since $x - z$ is assumed interior to $C^{*}$ we have

\[ x - z = \sum_{i = 1}^{l}a_{i}\alpha_{i}, \quad a_{i}> 0 . \]

Now let $\omega_{i}\in W$ be the reflexion in the face $\left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle = 0$ so that

\[ \omega_{i}x = x - 2 \left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle \alpha_{i}, \]

and define for $0 \leqslant t \leqslant 1$ constants

\[ b_{i}= t a_{i}/ 2 \left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle , \quad b = 1 - \sum b_{i}. \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned}b x + \sum b_{i}\omega_{i}x & = x + \sum b_{i}\left(\omega_{i}x - x\right) = x - 2 \sum b_{i}\left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle \alpha_{i}\\ & = x - t \sum a_{i}\alpha_{i}= t z + ( 1 - t ) x = y . \end{aligned}\]

Hence $y \in \widehat{W}x$ provided $b_{i}\geqslant 0$ and $b \geqslant 0$, and this will hold if

[?line conf=0.74: \[ 0 \leqslant t \leqslant 2 \left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle / l a_{i}\text{for all}i . \]]

It remains to examine the quantity

\[ c_{i}( x , z ) = 2 \left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle / l a_{i}, \]

when we vary $x$ on the interval $z x$. Replacing $x$ by the variable point $y = t z + ( 1 - t ) x , a_{i}$ gets

replaced by $( 1 - t ) a_{i}$ and

[?line conf=0.76: \[ \begin{aligned}\left\langle y , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle & = t \left\langle z , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle + ( 1 - t ) \left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle \\ & \geqslant ( 1 - t ) \left\langle x , \alpha_{i}\right\rangle \text{since}z \in C \quad \text{and}\quad t \geqslant 0 . \end{aligned}\]]

Hence $c_{i}( y , z ) \geqslant c_{i}( x , z )$ and the proof is completed by taking $c ( x , z ) = \min_{i}c_{i}( x , z )$.

[?line conf=0.78: Remark. The partial ordering $y \leqslant x$ for $x \in C$ is the usual ordering for dominant weights of]

representations, when we consider not the Lie algebra of $T$ but its dual. The reinterpretation in

terms of convex hulls of $W$-orbits is given in Adams (1969). In our case we are interested not in

representations but in conjugacy classes but the partial ordering is essentially the same.

Kostant (1973) proved the following generalization of the Schur-Horn theorem:

(12.15)

\[ \pi ( G y ) = \widehat{W}y \]

where $y \in \mathfrak{t}, \pi : \mathfrak{g}\rightarrow \mathfrak{t}$ is orthogonal projection and $G y$ denotes the $G$-orbit of $y$ under the adjoint

action. See also Atiyah (1982) for a different proof in a more general context. Using (12.15), or

rather the easier half that gives the inclusion $\pi ( G y ) \subset W y$, we shall now prove the promised result

about convex invariant functions:

[?line conf=0.83: Proposition 12.16. Let $\phi$ be a $W$-invariant convex function on t and $\psi$ the corresponding $G$-invariant]

function on $\mathfrak{g}$. Then $\psi$ is also convex.

Proof. For any function on $R^{n}$ it will be convenient to define $\Gamma ( f )$ to be the region 'above its

graph', i.e. all points $( x , y )$ with $x \in R^{n}, y \in R$ such that $y > f'( x )$. Convexity of the function $f$ is

then equivalent to convexity of $\Gamma ( f )$. Recall also that $\Gamma$ is convex if every boundary point $a$

has a supporting hyperplane $H_{a}$ (i.e. $\Gamma$ is contained in one of the two half-spaces complementary

to $H_{a}$ ). Now consider the functions $\phi , \psi$ and the corresponding regions

\[ \Gamma ( \phi ) \subset \mathfrak{t}\oplus R , \quad \Gamma ( \psi ) \subset \mathfrak{g}\oplus R . \]
