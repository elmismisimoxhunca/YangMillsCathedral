holomorphic and so is locally given by $w_{1}= \ldots = w_{n}= 0$ where $w_{i}$ are holomorphic

\[ \mathrm{d}w_{i}= \Sigma A_{i j}\left(\mathrm{~d}\lambda_{j}- \omega_{j k}\lambda_{k}\right) + \Sigma B_{i j}\mathrm{~d}z_{j}. \]

$\partial / \partial \bar{z}_{l}$ and find

\[ \nabla_{\bar{l}}\left(\left. \mathrm{~d}w_{i}\right|_{0}\right) = \left. \Sigma \left(\partial A_{i j}/ \partial \bar{z}_{l}\right) \mathrm{d}\lambda_{j}\right|_{0}+ \left. \Sigma A_{i j}\omega_{j k}^{\bar{l}}\mathrm{~d}\lambda_{k}\right|_{0}. \]

Remarks

1. Although the theorem is expressed in terms of connections on vector bundles,

it is equally true for principal bundles with compact structure group, for if $E$ has a

$G$-structure for $G \subset U ( k )$ which is preserved by the connection, then the holo-

[?line conf=0.73: morphic bundle $P \times{}_{U ( k )}G L ( k , \mathbb{C}) / G^{\mathrm{c}}$ has a covariant constant section where $P$ is]

[?line conf=0.90: the bundle of unitary frames of $E$. Since ∇ is a $( 1,0 )$ connection, every covariant]

constant section is holomorphic and hence the holomorphic frame bundle reduces

to $G^{\mathrm{c}}$. In the framework of principal $G^{\mathrm{c}}$-bundles the holomorphic structure can be

described geometrically as follows. Using the connection we split the tangent bundle

to the principal bundle into horizontal and vertical parts. Using the complex

[?line conf=0.82: structure of base ( $X$ ) and fibre ( $G^{\mathrm{c}}$ ) we then get an almost complex structure on the]

principal bundle. One can then check that the integrability for this almost complex

[?line conf=0.79: structure is the vanishing of the $( 0,2 )$-component of the curvature tensor. The]

complex structure is clearly invariant under the action of $G^{\mathrm{c}}$ and hence defines a

holomorphic bundle.

[?line conf=0.61: 2. The condition $\Omega \in A^{1,1}( \operatorname{End}E )$ is unchanged by a gauge transformation. For]

two gauge-equivalent connections, the two complex structures on $E$ are not the

same, but are equivalent under the gauge transformation.

3. If $\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}X = 1$, then $\Lambda^{2}= \Lambda^{1,1}$ so every hermitian connection on a $C^{\infty}$ vector

bundle over a Riemann surface defines a complex structure.

4. If $\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}X = 2$ and $X$ has a hermitian metric, then a bundle $E$ on the under-

lying real 4 -manifold is anti-self-dual iff $\Omega \in A_{-}^{2}$ (End $E$ ) $= A_{0}^{1,1}$ (End $E$ ) where

$A_{0}^{1,1}$ is the space of $( 1,1 )$ forms orthogonal to the hermitian form (see § 1). It follows

from the theorem, then, that each such bundle is holomorphic. Moreover, because

the curvature is orthogonal to the hermitian 2-form any holomorphic section of $E$ is

covariant constant by the vanishing theorem of Kobayashi \& Wu (1970).
